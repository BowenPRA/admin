A progress report is the one-page document each family receives for each child at the end of every quarter. This chapter covers the words used on it, the five levels, the three templates, who writes which part, the writing rules, translation into Vietnamese and printing. Open it two or three weeks before a quarter ends, when a teacher asks how the editor works, and before anyone changes a template or a limit.

## What a progress report is

- One report per enrolled student per quarter, made and written in The Current on **Teaching** → **Reports**. Its printed title is "Learning Progress Report" ("Báo cáo Tiến bộ Học tập" on the Vietnamese page).
- It is always one A4 page. A report set to "English and Vietnamese" also prints a separate Vietnamese page. The two languages are never on the same page.
- From top to bottom: the masthead (the logo on the left; on the right the title and one line with the quarter, the school year, the programme and the report date, standing on a blue-to-green rule that starts a little past the logo's wave, under "ACADEMY"); the student's photo (cropped to a circle) with the full name in large type, the nickname in quotes and the year group, beside the homeroom comment, a pale blue panel with a blue rule down its left side, which the homeroom teacher signs at the bottom right (a dash, then "Mr. Caleb"; on a line of its own, or at the end of the comment's last line when the box is tight); the academic areas; the review scores, where the year group has them, beside the learner skills ("How I learn"); the specialist and vocational areas; a band with the key to the five levels on the left (a line per level) and the two signature blocks on the right, each with the person's own signature above the line (from **Setup** → **Signatures**, since 23 October 2026) and the name and role below it; the closing message and the legal line at the foot.
- The design since 23 October 2026 (Bowen asked for a bolder, more polished page that keeps its colour): each section is a pale panel in its own colour under a solid title band, with no outline, and rows inside it are parted by thin lines. Each academic area starts with its icon in a white circle, above "Intensive English in", the class name, the teacher and the level, all centred in the name column. The icons are as large as the rows allow (up to 26pt, the same size in every row of a page); spare room on a page goes to them first, and where a page is too full for at least 14pt (Nursery and Kindergarten in Vietnamese with every box full) they are left off. A dark blue tile beside the lead line was tried the same night and turned down ("I wanted the icons over the text not to the side", "too dark"). Names, the title and the headings are in Be Vietnam Pro (the newsletter's typeface), comments in Roboto. The motto ("Learning for Life. Growing Together.") moved from under the logo to the footer (and off the page that afternoon, see below), and the scores table shows a light dash for a score still to come instead of "TBD". A lighter version, with white sections and small step meters for the levels, was tried that night and set aside as too plain. Later on 23 October Seth and Hien chose the five bubbles over the solid level pills (Lily's and Carrot's reports shown both ways): every level now prints as five bubbles, filled up to the level in its colour and white after it, in a pale capsule of that colour with the level's name ("●●●●○ Confident"), on the academic rows, the cards, How I Learn and the key. On Target fills three of the five; the key says it is the goal for this stage. Tested with every box at its limit and at 85%, in English and Vietnamese, for Nursery, Kindergarten and Years 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7: every page at exactly the text size it had before, nothing cut.
- Print polish since 22 October 2026: the homeroom teacher's name is the signature under the homeroom comment, no longer a "Homeroom:" line beside the photo; quotes and apostrophes print curly (“Chom chom”, Linnie’s), and the school year with an en dash (2026–2027); the closing message, when it needs two lines, breaks between its two sentences rather than leaving one word on the second line. None of this changes what teachers type. Bowen keeps the purple "Comment:" label on individual comments and the green "Topics covered:" on every course description, the academic rows included (tried without them and in blue that night).
- The foot of the page since the afternoon of 23 October 2026 (Bowen's ask): the motto is no longer printed. The closing message ("We celebrate learning in all its forms. Thank you for your partnership in …") took its place at the left of the footer, on one line beside the legal line, or two lines broken between the sentences when a long name leaves no room. The key to the levels moved from under the masthead to the band above the footer, a line per level (bubbles, name and description at 7pt, where the tiles had two lines at 5.9pt), beside the signature blocks, which now have the signature above the line and the typed name and role below it. The room this frees opens up the page: where a page has room to spare, the space between and inside the sections and the line spacing of the comments grow; a full page is spaced as before. Tested with every box at its limit and at 85%, in English and Vietnamese, for Nursery, Kindergarten and Years 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7, and with five real Quarter 1 pages: no page's text came out smaller than before, several came out larger (8pt to 8.25pt), nothing cut. A level name too long for its capsule in a narrow card ("Moving Beyond" with four cards in a row) now closes up the capsule or gives it a line of its own rather than being cut short.
- The logo since the same afternoon (Bowen's ask): 126pt wide (66pt before 23 October), set 12pt in from the left margin, at the height of the rule under the masthead (the rule's height falls across its green arcs and the top of its blue wave's crest), with the title and its line standing on the rule. For a few hours that afternoon it was 128pt wide with "ACADEMY" standing on the rule, then 112pt and 120pt with the rule through the wave; Bowen settled on this. Later the same day Bowen did not like the rule cutting through the logo: it now starts 4pt past the tip of the wave's crest, under "ACADEMY", so it never touches the logo's artwork (a first try started it after the whole logo, in line with the homeroom panel, and Bowen asked for it longer), 2pt thick (1.3pt before) with rounded ends, and runs from the light blue of the logo's wave through the brand blue to the green. Tested on every page above: no page's text size changed.
- The PDF a teacher sees in the editor's preview is the same file the family receives.
- Only Enrolled students get a report. Pending and Past students are not offered when reports are made.

## The words used

Reports follow the word rules in [Rules and wording](#/handbook/rules). Never write "report card" or "grades", and never call PRA a "school", on a report, in an email about reports or in a message to teachers. <!-- allow -->

| Word | What it means at PRA |
|---|---|
| Progress report | The one-page report for one student and one quarter. |
| Learning area | A subject or area on the report: Mathematics, Cooking, Wellbeing. |
| Quarter | The reporting period. Never "term": the editor flags it. |
| Level | One of PRA's five progress levels, below. |
| Assessment, score | Say "assessments" and "scores", not "tests". |
| Progress review | PRA's own quarterly assessment in an academic area. Its result is the review score. |
| Review score | The raw score typed in ("40/50") and the percentage worked out from it. |
| Class reference | The average of every review score entered for that area in the year group that quarter, the student's own included, printed beside the student's score. The Current works it out each time a report is shown or printed, so it is always up to date. |
| Summative | The end-of-year assessment. Its score appears on the last quarter's report only. |
| Course description | One short text per year group and learning area saying what the class covered. It prints as "topics covered". |
| Homeroom comment | The homeroom teacher's comment on the whole child. |
| Learner skills | The rated skills under "How I learn". |

## The five levels

Every learning area and every learner skill gets one of five levels. The key at the foot of every report, beside the signatures, prints each level with its bubbles and its description, so parents read the same words teachers chose from and see what each number of filled bubbles means.

| Printed order | Level | Code | Stored value | Vietnamese | Teachers see | Printed in the key |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emerging | E | 1 | Làm quen | With a lot of support | Beginning to develop the skills, with a lot of support. |
| 2 | Practicing | P | 2 | Luyện tập | With some help | Building understanding through practice, with some help. |
| 3 | On Target | O | 3 | Đạt mục tiêu | Independently: the goal | Secure and independent. The goal for this stage. |
| 4 | Confident | C | 5 | Vững vàng | Consistently and skilfully | Consistently strong, even in new situations. |
| 5 | Moving Beyond | M | 4 | Vượt mục tiêu | Beyond what is expected | Goes beyond what is expected; ready for extension. |

Until 23 October 2026 the Vietnamese names of Confident and Moving Beyond were "Tự tin" and "Nổi trội". "Tự tin" describes the child's character rather than their work, and "Nổi trội" ranks a child against the others; "Vững vàng" and "Vượt mục tiêu" sit beside "Đạt mục tiêu" as steps on one scale. Vietnamese staff have not read the new names yet (not checked since 23 October 2026).

### Why On Target is the goal

On Target sits in the middle of the scale, with two levels below it and two above. It means the student does what the stage expects, on their own. It is the target for every student, not a middling result. Confident and Moving Beyond are for students who go past it.

### Why the stored values run 1, 2, 3, 5, 4

The stored value is how a level is saved on each report. It is an ID, not a rank.

1. Until 21 September 2026 the scale had four levels: Emerging (1), Practicing (2), Confident (3), Moving Beyond (4).
2. On 21 September 2026 a fifth level, "Accomplished", was added between Confident and Moving Beyond. It took the next free value, 5.
3. On 22 September 2026 Bowen renamed the levels. Value 3 became On Target, the goal. Value 5 became Confident.

A level a teacher entered as the old "Confident" therefore reads On Target now, which is what it meant.

> **Rule:** never change a level's stored value once reports use it. To rename a level, change its name in Report settings and leave the value alone.

### Turning a score into a level

There is no center-wide rule for this. As an example, these are the bands Bowen used for his own Year 7 Mathematics and Science in Quarter 1 2026:

| Score | Level |
|---|---|
| 93% and above | Moving Beyond |
| 85% to 92% | Confident |
| 65% to 84% | On Target |
| 40% to 64% | Practicing |
| Under 40% | Emerging |

### The three tiers

| Tier | On each student's report | What the teacher writes |
|---|---|---|
| Academic | Level, review score (where the year group has scores), an individual comment | A comment for each student, plus one course description for the year group |
| Specialist | Level and an individual comment | A comment for each student |
| Vocational | Level | One course description for the year group, no individual comment |

A vocational area can be ticked in Report settings to take an individual comment instead of the course description. It still prints with the other vocational areas. Early Years Physical Development is the only one.

A vocational area can instead have a **Fixed course description** in Report settings. That text prints on every report with the area, every quarter, under "Topics covered:" like the other course descriptions, and teachers only pick a level. Movement is the only one (since 22 October 2026; trimmed that evening to about 250 characters, the length of the others). Leave the box empty for a description written each quarter.

Such an area can still be a specialist area in some year groups: **Specialist area instead in** in Report settings, under the fixed description, ticks them. Movement is ticked for Year 7 only: Mr. Caleb writes a comment on each Year 7 student, as before 22 October, while Mr. Chiến's Years 1 to 6 keep the fixed description. Comments typed before a change like this stay saved and print again once the year group is ticked.

On the Teachers page, the Reports page and the editor, a violet speech-bubble icon means "comments to write" and a green page icon means "a course description to write". An academic area has both. An area with a fixed description shows a grey "Level only" tag.

### Partial-day students

A student who arrives after the morning lessons has an arrival time on their record (Students → the student → Enrollment → partial-day arrival time). The morning is when the academic lessons happen, so a partial-day student's report has no academic areas. The editor's header says "Partial day, from" and the time. This is intended. On the printed page the time shows under the year group ("Afternoons from 11:35"), and since the page has less to say, each section grows only a little and the rest is left as white space above the level key and signatures.

### Upper Secondary

| Who | What they may change |
|---|---|
| A teacher | The learning areas ticked for them on the Teachers page, year group by year group: level, comment, review score, course description. For their homeroom year groups also the homeroom comment, the learner skills, the report's language and its status. |

- A teacher's rights come from their row on **Setup** → **Teachers** (head only). The database enforces them, so a part a teacher may not edit cannot be saved, whatever the screen shows.
- The homeroom teacher's name on the report comes from the Schedule. The right to write the homeroom parts comes from the Teachers row. The two must agree: "Who teaches what" on the Teachers page flags a homeroom teacher on the Schedule who cannot yet write the homeroom parts.
- A teacher with no row sees "You are not linked to any classes yet, so reports are read-only."
- Statuses: **Draft**, **Ready to review**, **Published**. The homeroom teacher moves a report to Ready to review. Only the head publishes, and once a report is published only the head can change it.

## The cycle each quarter

### Two weeks before the quarter ends

1. **Setup** → **Teachers** (head): read "Who teaches what". Every learning area of every year group needs a teacher, and every homeroom needs its teacher ticked. Fix gaps with the ticks on each teacher's row.
2. **Teaching** → **Schedule**: check the homeroom teacher of each class. The report header and the Homeroom Teacher signature line take the name from here.
3. **Students**: check that every child who should get a report is Enrolled and in the right year group, with the right legal first name, the right partial-day time and a photo for progress reports.

### Making the reports

1. **Reports** → **Create reports** (head). The window "Create progress reports" asks for the **Period**, the **Template** and the **Year groups** (each shows how many enrolled students it has). For a child who joined late, use **One student only (optional)** instead.
2. Read the summary line. It names each year group's homeroom teacher from the Schedule and says how many reports will be made. Students who already have a report for that quarter are skipped. From Quarter 2 on, each student's levels from their latest earlier report this year are copied in as the previous levels.
3. Press **Create N**.
4. Tell the teachers that comments are open.

> **Ask Claude:** "Write Gmail drafts in the admin@ inbox to each teacher saying the Quarter 2 progress report comments are open in The Current at https://current.pra.edu.vn/#/reports, what each of them writes, the character limits, and that everything must be ready to review by [date]. CC Seth. Sign them from me."

### Writing

- Teachers open **Reports** (it starts on **My classes**) or **Your reports** on the Home page. The **Still to write** card lists their unwritten course descriptions on the left and their students on the right.
- Every change saves by itself as it is typed. "Saved ✓" shows for a moment, then "All changes saved" stays until the next edit. A save that fails is tried again; if it keeps failing, the indicator says so. Only the changed boxes are saved, so two teachers writing on one report at the same time do not overwrite each other. Leaving the page saves anything still waiting.
- When the homeroom teacher thinks the report is complete, they set its status at the top of the editor to **Ready to review**.

### Reviewing, publishing, printing

1. The head opens **Reports**. The **Ready to review** tile counts the reports waiting. Each year group's card shows its completion bar and "n ready to review · n in draft".
2. The head reads each report in the editor with **Preview** open and corrects what needs it.
3. On each year group that has review scores, the head checks that every score is in. The class reference beside each score is the average of all the scores entered for that area in the year group that quarter, the student's own included. The Current works it out each time a report is shown or printed, so a late or corrected score changes every report's class reference at once, and the editor shows it under the score ("Class average 82%, from 10 scores in Year 7 so far"). There is nothing to press. Until 24 October 2026 the head pressed a **Class references** button and the figure was saved on each report; the saved figures are no longer used, which is why the Quarter 1 English figures printed before that date differ from report to report.
4. Reports set to English and Vietnamese get their Vietnamese now (see [Vietnamese reports](#/handbook/reports?s=vietnamese-reports) below).
5. The head sets each report's status to **Published**. A published report is locked for everyone but the head.
6. **Print all** on each year group card opens one PDF with every report of that year group, one page per student, in English. When some reports are set to English and Vietnamese, the button reads **Print all (English)** and a second button, **Tiếng Việt (n)**, makes the Vietnamese PDF for those n reports.
7. Save each PDF from the print page. The Super Admin's **Setup** → **Settings** → **Export to a folder** also writes every published report as a PDF into `Reports/2026-2027/Quarter 1/Year 3/` and so on, inside the export folder. That is the off-site copy (see [Behind the scenes](#/handbook/behind)).
8. The PDFs go to the families. The sources do not record whether Quarter 1 2026 went out printed, by email or both (not checked since 15 October 2026). Ask Seth how he wants it done.

## Writing a report

### The editor

- The bar at the top: the student's name, year group, quarter and completion; the save indicator; the status menu (homeroom teacher and head); **Show Tiếng Việt** on a bilingual report; **Preview** on a wide screen; **PDF**, or **English PDF** and **Tiếng Việt PDF**.
- The **Writing comments** box gives the legal first name with a copy button, and the rules below. **Hide guidelines** folds it away.
- Each text box has a counter with its limit and its minimum, and the wording checker's advice underneath. The browser's spell check is on.
- On a report set to English and Vietnamese, the Vietnamese box sits beside each English one.
- The report's language is in its **Overview** card: **Reports** → "English only" or "English and Vietnamese".
- Text written before 24 September 2026 in the boxes that were removed that day (next focus, "in their own words", experiences) shows as a grey "no longer printed" note, so a teacher can move it into a comment. Those boxes no longer print.

### Names

- Use the student's legal first name, never the nickname, with the Vietnamese accents where the name has them. The printed header shows the full name and, under it, the nickname in quotes. The nickname is left out when it is already part of the name (Anaya Bisht, "Anaya"), accents and capitals aside. A full name too long for one line breaks before the first name ("Bùi Nguyễn" / "Nhã Vy"), or else where the two lines come out most even.
- As a report prints, small slips are tidied without changing what is saved: double spaces, a space before a comma or full stop, and a missing full stop at the end of a comment or course description.
- For a Vietnamese name, the first name is the last two words of the full name: the full name starts with the family name, and the middle and given names together are the first name.
- The legal first name is on the student record: **Students** → the student → **Personal** → legal first name used on reports. If it is blank, The Current works it out from the full name. Check that guess for every name that is not in the usual Vietnamese form before reports are made.

### The wording checker

Under each box, as the teacher types, the checker gives advice. It never blocks a save. It flags:

- the words PRA never uses, listed under "The words used" above;
- "tests" (say "assessments") and "term" as a period of time (say "quarter"; "long-term" and "in terms of" are fine);
- the nickname written instead of the legal first name;
- a Vietnamese name typed without its accents;
- in Vietnamese text, the Vietnamese words for the same things (see [Rules and wording](#/handbook/rules)).

### Character limits

| Box | Most characters | Fewest to count as written |
|---|---|---|
| Homeroom comment | 500 | 400 |
| Academic comment | 750 | 650 |
| Academic course description ("topics covered") | 120 | no minimum |
| Specialist comment | 550 | 450 |
| Early Years Applied English, Cooking and Physical Development comments | 600 | 300 |
| Vocational course description | 250 | 150 |

- Every character counts, spaces included.
- Nothing is ever blocked. Below its minimum, the counter turns amber ("too short: at least N to count as written") and the part counts as not written: it stays on **Still to write** and the Home page's task list, and it is left out of the completion percentage.
- Above the limit, the counter shows it, and the PDF sets the text smaller and then cuts the end (a red outline in the preview).

> **Rule:** the limits are never lowered. Every template was measured with every box at its limit, in English and in Vietnamese, and it still fits one page. If a report ever needs more room, the layout is changed and measured again. The numbers stay.

### How Bowen writes a comment

These are Bowen's rules for his own subject comments. They are a good model for any teacher who asks.

- Open with something positive and end on encouragement. The whole comment stays warm.
- Put areas to grow as next steps and habits, with concrete things to do. Never as faults.
- Tie the comment to English wherever possible, anywhere in the text. Mathematics and Science are taught in English to students for whom English is a second language, so English is often the real barrier. For example: strong in discussion, and the next step is getting those ideas into written answers.
- Never write "next quarter". Write "going forward", or keep it general.
- Use the concrete details the teacher gives: what the student enjoyed, what improved.
- Aim close to the limit: an academic comment between 650 and 750 characters, nearer 750.
- The score prints in its own table, so the comment does not repeat the number. It may explain it, for example blanks left on the assessment.
- Let the words match the level. A comment for an Emerging level reads a little less optimistic than one for Confident.
- Plain copy: say it once, no flourish at the end.
- Check whether the student is a boy or a girl before a pronoun is used, even in a draft.

Claude can shape a comment from notes. Claude cannot sign in to The Current, so the finished text is pasted into the editor by a person. (For his own Year 7 class in October 2026 Bowen had Claude put the comments in with a database file instead, which he then ran in Supabase like any other.)

> **Ask Claude:** "Here are my notes on a Year 7 student's Science this quarter: [notes]. She is a girl. Write the progress report comment in Bowen's comment style, between 650 and 750 characters, and tell me the exact count."

## The PDF

- One A4 page, always. English and Vietnamese are separate PDFs. Bowen's words, 15 September 2026: "I WANT ONE PAGE REPORTS."
- The PDF is made in the browser, with real text that can be selected. The same file is the editor's preview, the print page and the download.
- Each box has a fixed place on the page. Text that is too long for its box is first set a little smaller, then cut with "…".
- The preview marks problems on screen only; the marks never print:

| Mark | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Red outline | The box is too full and the end is cut off. The note names the boxes. | Shorten the text in that box. |
| Amber outline (Vietnamese page) | This part has no Vietnamese yet and prints in English. | Translate it (next section). |
| Grey note "set a little smaller than usual" | The comments are a size smaller to fit. Nothing is cut. | Nothing to do. |

- The file name holds the student's name, the quarter, the academic year and the language.
- The photo is the student record's "Photo for progress reports", cropped to a circle.
- The signatures are the ones saved under **Setup** → **Signatures**: the homeroom teacher's (from the Schedule) and the Head of Academy's, each matched by title and name ("Mr. Caleb", "Ms. Thắm N"). They print above the signature line, with the typed name and role below it, redrawn a little bolder in dark ink so they read as pen on paper. Someone without a saved signature gets the typed name only. Office accounts and head teachers can read every signature, so their PDFs show both; a teacher can read only their own, so a teacher's preview shows only theirs. A new signature reaches the PDFs within about five minutes.

> **Rule:** a report never spills onto a second page. If one ever does, it is a fault in the layout. Ask Claude to fix it, and to test every template with every box at its limit in both languages before deploying.

## Vietnamese reports

A report is translated when its language is set to "English and Vietnamese". The translation is done in the Claude desktop app, on the Claude plan. The Current never sends report text anywhere.

> **Rule:** no Claude API key, ever, and no paid API billing. Bowen chose this on 15 September 2026 and repeated it on 1 October 2026. There is no "translate" button that calls Claude; the file goes through the desktop app by hand.

### The translation file

1. **Reports** → **Translate** (head and Super Admin). The window is "Translate reports with Claude".
2. Step 1, **Download the English**: choose the period and year group. By default the file holds only the parts that still have no Vietnamese. Press **Download file**. It is a JSON file of the English text, with the rules, a glossary and each part's character limit inside it. Since 23 October 2026 (file version 2) it also gives the teacher who wrote each part, so Claude writes "thầy" or "cô", and the pronoun for the child ("bé" in Nursery and Kindergarten, "em" from Year 1), and its rules are the ones used for the first Vietnamese reports (below).
3. Step 2, in the Claude desktop app: start a new chat, attach the file, and send the message the window gives (its **Copy** button copies it). Claude returns a file with the Vietnamese filled in. Download it.
4. Step 3, **Upload Claude's file**: **Choose file** and pick it. The app lists every part with what it found: over the character limit, English changed since the file was made, not found, a word on the banned list, the nickname used.
5. Untick anything that looks wrong. Existing Vietnamese is replaced only if you choose that.
6. Press **Fill in N parts**. The app reads each report again before writing, so nothing else on it is overwritten.

### The review step

- Open each bilingual report's Vietnamese page (**Tiếng Việt PDF**). Amber outlines mean parts still in English.
- A Vietnamese-speaking member of staff reads the Vietnamese pages before they are published. The first Vietnamese written by Claude for the website read as awkward to staff (30 September 2026), and reports go to the same families. The rules for natural Vietnamese are in [Rules and wording](#/handbook/rules).
- Corrections are typed straight into the Vietnamese boxes in the editor.

**Reports** → **Settings** opens "Report settings" (head only). A change there is live as soon as it is saved; no deploy is needed.

| Part | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Wording | The document title in English and Vietnamese, the tagline (the motto; not printed on the report since 23 October 2026), the legal line at the foot, the closing message in both languages |
| Academic year and periods | The year label and each quarter's name, start and end |

- The settings are saved as one document. If two people edit Report settings at the same time, the last to save wins and the other's changes are lost. Edit them one person at a time.
- Teacher names on reports come from the Teachers page, not from settings. A name typed on a report is used only while nobody is linked to that area.

A name ending in a single capital letter after a space loses it; the screens keep it.
- **Helping teachers** (Report settings, since 22 October 2026: Ms. Thắm V) are left off a card they share with another teacher, so Technology and Art & Crafts in Years 2 to 5 print "Mr. Caleb" alone. Where a helping teacher is the only one on an area, they print. The Teachers page and her rights to the area do not change.

### The settings version

- The defaults for these settings are in the code. The saved settings carry a version number: 17 since 23 October 2026 (version 17 renamed a "Head Teacher" signature line to "Head of Academy"). Version 16 replaced Vietnamese wording still at its earlier default (two level names and descriptions, the program lines, the closing message, six learner skills, the "Tiếng Anh nâng cao" lead); wording changed by hand is left alone.
- When Claude adds a learning area or a template in the code, it raises the version. The first time the new app reads the saved settings, it upgrades them: versions 14 and 15 gave the "Intensive English in ..." names to every area still under an earlier default name (a name changed by hand is left alone) and dropped an Early Years "Mathematics", versions 10 to 13 (22 October 2026) made Movement a fixed description, renamed History and the vocational classes, shortened Movement's description and gave Year 7 Movement its comments back, version 9 gave Physical Development an individual comment and added Cooking to Early Years, version 8 added Master Minds, version 7 renamed a standard four- or five-level scale to the current names (a scale changed by hand is left alone), version 5 added the Early Years template.
- Pressing **Save** in Report settings stores the upgraded version.
- The upgrade adds the area to the template. Reports made before it do not have it until the head presses **Add … to N reports** on the year group's card.

## What can go wrong

| What you see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A teacher cannot type in a box | Their Teachers row does not tick that area for that year group, or the report is published | The head ticks it on **Setup** → **Teachers**, or sets the report back from Published |
| "You are not linked to any classes yet" | The teacher has no Teachers row, or it has no ticks | The head adds or fixes the row |
| The wrong homeroom teacher prints | The Schedule names someone else for that class | Correct the class on **Teaching** → **Schedule**; reports follow it |
| A comment is written but still listed under Still to write | It is shorter than its minimum | Write more, up to the limit |
| Red outline in the preview | Too much text for the box; the end is cut | Shorten that box |
| Amber outline on the Vietnamese page | A part has no Vietnamese | Run the translation file again, or type it in |
| A new learning area is missing from this quarter's reports | The reports were made before it was added | **Add … to N reports** on the year group card |
| A Year 1 report has no Master Minds card | The area has not been added to that year group's reports, or Master Minds has no teacher | **Add … to N reports**, and tick Master Minds for its teacher on the Teachers page |
| A Nursery or Kindergarten report has no Cooking card, or no teacher on it | Cooking has not been added to that year group's reports, or nobody is ticked for it | **Add … to N reports**, and give Mr. Seth Cooking for Nursery and Kindergarten on the Teachers page (**Add … new classes from the schedule**, since 19 October 2026) |
| The class reference looks wrong | A score in the year group is missing, typed wrong, or typed as a percentage that does not match its raw score | Check the year group's scores in the editor; the reference follows them by itself |
| A child is missing from Create reports | The student is Pending or Past, or in another year group | Fix the record on **Students**, then create that one student's report |
| The Reports list shows "now Year X" beside a student | The student moved year group after the report was made | Decide with Seth which year group the report should be for |
| "Permission denied" or reports will not save at all | A database file has not been run, or a rule is missing | See [Behind the scenes](#/handbook/behind) |
| A report was deleted | Deleting a report deletes all its parts. There is no undo. | Create that student's report again and rewrite it |

> **Careful:** two database files for reports are not confirmed as run on the live database: the Early Years teacher links of 21 September 2026 and Master Minds with Mr. Chiến's Movement of 2 October 2026. Each has a click-through alternative on the Teachers and Reports pages. [Known problems and open items](#/handbook/open) keeps the list.
